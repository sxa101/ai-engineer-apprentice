# Skill: take-notes

Everything written into `learner/notes/`. One file per lesson, numbered in course order.

## The privacy rule first

This is a privacy rule, not a formatting preference, and it does not get relaxed for a deadline.

- The notes folder is the learner's own working out. It is not evidence, not an appendix, and not
  something an assessor is entitled to. Notes are where they get things wrong privately.
- **Never attach notes to a submission**, never paste them into a submission box, never bundle them
  into a docx that gets uploaded. Not a subset, not "just the relevant one".
- If a reflection looks thin without the notes, the reflection needs writing better. It does not get
  propped up with private material.
- They are never pushed to any remote either. In this workspace they live under `learner/`, which is
  gitignored. Keep it that way.

## How notes get written

- Straight after the learner answers a retrieval question, while the answer is still in their head.
- **In their voice.** Short, clear, a bit rough, slightly imperfect on purpose. It records what they
  could actually explain, not what the page said.
- **Wrong answers go in next to the corrections.** The pair is worth more a week later than a clean
  summary, because it shows exactly where the understanding broke.
- "Activity:" units have no text box on the platform - it says record your answers in a format of
  your choice. The deliverable is the notes file and the answers in it are the learner's.
- Notes are never overwritten, trimmed or tidied into polished prose later. When asked for the notes,
  hand them back as they stand. They are the revision material.
- Commentary the learner heard but the page does not show (analogies, asides, worked examples given
  aloud) goes in too - it is part of the lesson and it exists nowhere else.

## Who drafts them

The assistant writes the notes, in full, in the learner's voice, at block close - it does not ask
how the learner wants to proceed. Drafting notes is one of the assistant's original responsibilities
in this course. The learner's job is to review and correct, not to co-write. Presenting the finished
file as "notes are written, ready for your review" is the expected hand-off.

## When writing them

- During the block, per unit, before the tick. Marking before the notes exist throws the explanation
  away and the notes end up a transcript of the page instead of something the learner owns.
- A second pass over a finished notes file may correct wording, but keeps the corrections visible.
  The record of what happened stays honest.
