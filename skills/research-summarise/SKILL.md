# Skill: research-summarise

Gather material, verify it, and hand it back at the learner's register. Used for activity briefs that
ask the learner to look something up, and for background the lesson assumes.

## Procedure

1. **Pin the question.** Restate what is actually being asked in one sentence before searching. An
   activity brief often asks three small things, not one big one - answer each in its own place.
2. **Gather from real sources.** Product docs, standards, papers, the vendor's own pages. For claims
   that matter, confirm against a second independent source. Marketing copy is a source for what a
   vendor claims, never for what is true.
3. **Extract the specifics.** Names, versions, dates, numbers. A summary that loses the specifics is
   a vibe, not research.
4. **Summarise at the learner's register.** Short sentences, ordinary words, slightly imperfect.
   The banned word list applies. If a sentence would not come out of the learner's mouth, rewrite it.
5. **Say what was not found.** An honest gap ("could not confirm whether X is supported on the free
   tier") is more useful than a smooth paragraph that papers over it.

## Output

- Into the relevant `learner/notes/` file for lesson work, or a dedicated section for an activity
  answer set. Never into a submission directly - that path goes through make-reflections and then
  human review.
- Cite where each fact came from, as a plain link or naming the page. The learner should be able to
  retrace the research.

## Rules

- The register rules from the starter prompt apply to research write-ups exactly as they do to
  reflections. Research is not an excuse for corporate voice.
- Retrieval still applies. After the write-up, one question on what was just researched, from memory.
- Time box it. Research expands to fill the block; the block has other things to do.
